🔥 High-Yield Bite List

Warfarin reversal

Minor: hold + oral vitamin K. Severe: hold + IV vitamin K + blood/FFP/PCC/clotting factors.

DOAC reversal

Dabigatran → idarucizumab. Apixaban/rivaroxaban/edoxaban → andexanet alfa.

Fibrinolytic bleeding

Bleeding is most common; fibrinolytics are for ischemic stroke/STEMI indications, not UA/NSTEMI.

Pregnancy anticoag

Use heparins/LMWH; avoid warfarin.

Thiazide calcium

Thiazides retain calcium; loops waste calcium.

Mannitol ICP

IV only; pulls water out of cells; watch HF/renal impairment/pulmonary edema.

Acetazolamide altitude

Bicarb wasting counters respiratory alkalosis; causes metabolic acidosis, stones; avoid cirrhosis.

Pregnancy HTN

Nifedipine XL/amlodipine, methyldopa, labetalol; avoid ACEi/ARB/aliskiren.

ACEi cough

Bradykinin buildup. Switch to ARB if cough.

Nitrate sildenafil

Too much cGMP → severe hypotension; wait sildenafil 24 hr/tadalafil 48 hr.

Amiodarone

Half-life weeks; iodine; pulmonary/liver/thyroid; digoxin dose ↓50%; increases warfarin/statin effects.

Statins

HMG-CoA reductase → ↑LDL receptors; pregnancy CI; myopathy DDIs.

PCSK9

-umab mAbs bind PCSK9 and preserve LDL receptors; inclisiran prevents PCSK9 production.

HFrEF pillars

SGLT2i, ARNI/ACEi/ARB, beta blocker, MRA. Avoid non-DHP CCBs.

DHP vs non-DHP

DHP = vessels/-dipine/edema. Non-DHP = heart/verapamil+diltiazem/bradycardia/constipation/HFrEF CI.

Beta1 selectivity

Metoprolol/atenolol/bisoprolol; selectivity fades at higher doses.

Last-Minute Mnemonics That Actually Help

  • Some Block Potassium Channels: Class I sodium, Class II beta blockers, Class III potassium, Class IV calcium.
  • DHP = DiPINE = Pressure/Pipes: vessels, edema/flushing/headache. Non-DHP = Node: verapamil/diltiazem, bradycardia/AV block/constipation, HFrEF contraindication.
  • Thiazides keep Calcium; Loops Lose Calcium.
  • Warfarin = vitamin K factors 2,7,9,10 + INR + bridge + teratogenic.
  • DOAC reversal: Dabi = Idaru; Xa-ban = Andexanet.
  • Amiodarone is the whale shark: huge half-life, huge tissue distribution, huge DDI list.
  • HFrEF Fantastic Four: SGLT2i, ARNI/ACEi/ARB, evidence beta blocker, MRA.

Q1ASA MOA

A reef diver takes aspirin for secondary prevention after MI. What is aspirin's key antiplatelet MOA?

Answer: B. Irreversibly inhibits platelet COX-1, decreasing TXA2
Aspirin irreversibly acetylates platelet COX-1. Platelets cannot synthesize new COX, so the effect lasts for the life of the platelet.
Q2ASA duration

A shark-bite victim on aspirin asks why bleeding risk persists after the last dose. Best explanation?

Answer: D. Platelets cannot make new COX enzyme
The drug level is not the issue; platelet function remains altered until new platelets are made.
Q3ASA DDI

Which counseling point is most testable for a patient taking daily low-dose aspirin who also uses ibuprofen?

Answer: B. Take aspirin 1 hour before or 8 hours after ibuprofen
Ibuprofen can antagonize aspirin's access to COX-1 if timed wrong.
Q4ASA CI

Which patient is a poor candidate for aspirin because of a classic age-related toxicity concern?

Answer: A. A 14-year-old with a viral illness
Avoid aspirin in patients under 19 with viral syndromes because of Reye syndrome risk.
Q5P2Y12 MOA

A platelet drug blocks ADP from activating a receptor so GP IIb/IIIa does not activate. Which class?

Answer: D. P2Y12 receptor antagonist
P2Y12 blockers stop ADP-mediated platelet activation and downstream GP IIb/IIIa activation.
Q6Clopidogrel DDI

A patient on clopidogrel starts omeprazole and has reduced antiplatelet response. Why?

Answer: B. Omeprazole inhibits CYP2C19 activation of clopidogrel
Clopidogrel is a prodrug requiring CYP2C19 conversion.
Q7Prasugrel CI

Which P2Y12 inhibitor is contraindicated in a patient with history of TIA/stroke?

Answer: A. Prasugrel
Prasugrel has a major bleeding concern and is contraindicated with prior TIA/stroke.
Q8Ticagrelor AE

A patient on ticagrelor complains of shortness of breath and the exam hints at increased adenosine. Which drug is responsible?

Answer: D. Ticagrelor
Ticagrelor can cause dyspnea because it increases adenosine effects.
Q9Ticagrelor DDI

Which counseling point is unique to ticagrelor?

Answer: D. Avoid aspirin doses over 100 mg because effectiveness may decrease
Ticagrelor has a boxed warning about reduced effectiveness with high-dose aspirin.
Q10PAR-1

A drug reversibly inhibits PAR-1, the platelet thrombin receptor, and is used in select patients with prior MI/PAD. Which drug?

Answer: D. Vorapaxar
Vorapaxar is the PAR-1 antagonist. Its use is limited by severe bleeding risk.
Q11GPIIbIIIa

A PCI patient receives an IV antiplatelet that blocks GP IIb/IIIa, preventing fibrinogen cross-linking. Which pair fits?

Answer: B. Eptifibatide or tirofiban
GP IIb/IIIa inhibitors block the final common platelet aggregation step.
Q12GPIIbIIIa AE

Which adverse effect is especially testable for GP IIb/IIIa inhibitors?

Answer: D. Acute thrombocytopenia and bleeding
These IV antiplatelets can cause thrombocytopenia and bleeding.
Q13PDE antiplatelet CI

Which antiplatelet is a PDE inhibitor that should be avoided in heart failure?

Answer: A. Cilostazol
Cilostazol is a PDE inhibitor with a heart failure contraindication.
Q14Dipyridamole CI

A patient with unstable angina is prescribed dipyridamole. What is the problem?

Answer: B. Dipyridamole should be avoided in unstable angina
Dipyridamole is not a major CAD antiplatelet and is avoided in unstable angina.
Q15Big distinction

Which drug-class comparison is most accurate?

Answer: C. Antiplatelets prevent platelet plug formation; anticoagulants reduce fibrin formation
Antiplatelets are platelet plug drugs; anticoagulants target fibrin/clotting cascade growth.
Q16UFH MOA

Unfractionated heparin binds antithrombin and primarily inhibits which targets?

Answer: C. Factor IIa/thrombin and Factor Xa
UFH has enough chain length to bridge antithrombin to thrombin and Xa.
Q17UFH monitoring

Which lab is classically used to monitor unfractionated heparin?

Answer: D. aPTT
UFH is monitored with activated partial thromboplastin time.
Q18HIT

A patient on heparin develops thrombocytopenia plus thrombosis. What immune target explains it?

Answer: D. IgG against heparin-PF4 complex
HIT is a hypercoagulable platelet-activating reaction to heparin-PF4 complexes.
Q19HIT management

What is the correct first step in suspected HIT?

Answer: A. Discontinue heparin
Stop heparin immediately and use a non-heparin anticoagulant if anticoagulation is needed.
Q20UFH reversal

Which reversal agent best matches unfractionated heparin bleeding?

Answer: C. Protamine sulfate
Protamine is positively charged and complexes with negatively charged heparin; it works best for UFH.
Q21LMWH MOA

Compared with UFH, LMWH is more selective for which clotting factor?

Answer: A. Factor Xa
Shorter heparin chains cannot bridge thrombin as efficiently, so LMWH has more anti-Xa activity.
Q22LMWH monitoring

Which monitoring exception for LMWH is most testable?

Answer: D. Check anti-Xa in renal impairment, pregnancy, or obesity
LMWH usually does not need routine coag monitoring, but anti-Xa can matter in special populations.
Q23Pregnancy anticoag

Why are heparins preferred for anticoagulation in pregnancy?

Answer: A. They are large molecules and do not cross the placenta
Heparin/LMWH are considered safest anticoagulants in pregnancy because they do not cross the placenta.
Q24Fondaparinux

Which anticoagulant is sometimes called synthetic heparin, selectively inhibits Xa, and has no reversal agent in the lecture?

Answer: B. Fondaparinux
Fondaparinux is a synthetic pentasaccharide Xa inhibitor; bleeding has no specific reversal agent in the slide set.
Q25Fondaparinux use

Fondaparinux is preferred in a patient with which history?

Answer: C. History of HIT
HIT is much less likely with fondaparinux, but severe renal impairment is a contraindication.
Q26Argatroban

An IV direct thrombin inhibitor is selected for a patient with HIT and hepatic dose concerns. Which drug?

Answer: A. Argatroban
Argatroban is IV/direct thrombin and used in HIT; reduce dose in hepatic impairment.
Q27Bivalirudin

Which IV direct thrombin inhibitor is used during PCI in patients with or at risk of HIT?

Answer: A. Bivalirudin
Bivalirudin is an IV direct thrombin inhibitor often associated with PCI anticoagulation.
Q28Warfarin MOA

Warfarin's MOA is best described as inhibition of which enzyme?

Answer: A. Vitamin K epoxide reductase (VKORC1)
Warfarin prevents regeneration of reduced vitamin K needed for clotting factor activation.
Q29Warfarin factors

Which clotting factors become under-carboxylated with warfarin?

Answer: B. II, VII, IX, X
Vitamin K dependent factors are II, VII, IX, and X.
Q30Warfarin bridge

Why does warfarin often require bridging with LMWH or heparin?

Answer: D. It takes days to reduce already-circulating clotting factors
Warfarin affects synthesis of new factors; it does not remove active factors already present.
Q31Warfarin monitoring

Which lab is used to monitor warfarin therapy?

Answer: D. INR
Warfarin is monitored with INR because it affects the extrinsic/common pathway via vitamin K factor changes.
Q32Warfarin reversal

Minor warfarin bleeding is generally managed by holding warfarin and giving which agent?

Answer: A. Oral vitamin K
The lecture separates minor bleeding from severe bleeding: minor = stop warfarin plus oral vitamin K.
Q33Warfarin reversal

Severe warfarin-associated bleeding requires IV vitamin K plus what supportive/replacement therapy?

Answer: C. Whole blood, frozen plasma, or clotting factor concentrates
Severe bleeding needs reversal and clotting factor replacement/support.
Q34Warfarin pregnancy

Which patient should not receive warfarin?

Answer: B. Pregnant patient
Warfarin is teratogenic and pregnancy category X in the lecture.
Q35Warfarin AE

Purple toe syndrome and skin necrosis point toward which anticoagulant?

Answer: B. Warfarin
Those are memorable warfarin adverse effects.
Q36Warfarin DDI

Which drug increases warfarin bleeding risk by inhibiting metabolism and is also a classic antiarrhythmic?

Answer: B. Amiodarone
Amiodarone is a high-yield warfarin DDI that increases anticoagulant effect/bleeding.
Q37Warfarin counseling

Which dietary counseling point is correct for warfarin?

Answer: D. Keep vitamin K intake consistent
Consistency matters; sudden changes in leafy greens can alter INR.
Q38DOAC MOA

Which DOAC directly inhibits thrombin rather than Factor Xa?

Answer: A. Dabigatran
Dabigatran is the only DOAC in this set that targets thrombin.
Q39Xa DOAC

Which suffix should make you think direct oral Factor Xa inhibitor?

Answer: C. -xaban
Apixaban, rivaroxaban, and edoxaban end in -xaban and directly inhibit Xa.
Q40DOAC reversal

Which reversal agent immediately neutralizes dabigatran?

Answer: D. Idarucizumab
Idarucizumab binds free and thrombin-bound dabigatran.
Q41DOAC reversal

Which reversal agent is a decoy/variant Factor Xa used for Factor Xa inhibitors?

Answer: D. Andexanet alfa
Andexanet binds Xa inhibitors with high affinity so they cannot bind native Factor Xa.
Q42Dabigatran CI

Which DOAC should be avoided in mechanical prosthetic heart valves?

Answer: B. Dabigatran
Dabigatran is contraindicated with mechanical prosthetic valves.
Q43Rivaroxaban counseling

Rivaroxaban doses greater than 15 mg should be taken how?

Answer: B. With the evening meal
Taking higher-dose rivaroxaban with the evening meal maximizes absorption.
Q44DOAC counseling

A DOAC patient stops therapy abruptly and develops thrombosis. What counseling was missed?

Answer: A. Do not abruptly discontinue DOACs because thrombosis risk increases
Abrupt discontinuation of DOACs can increase thrombotic events.
Q45Fibrinolytic MOA

Fibrinolytics are best described as drugs that do what?

Answer: C. Convert plasminogen to plasmin to degrade fibrin
Thrombolytics/fibrinolytics cleave plasminogen into plasmin, which breaks down fibrin.
Q46Fibrinolytic use

Which clinical situation is appropriate for fibrinolytic therapy in the lecture?

Answer: D. Ischemic stroke or STEMI when indicated
The lecture emphasizes ischemic stroke and STEMI alternatives to PCI, not unstable angina/NSTEMI.
Q47Fibrinolytic bleeding

A fibrinolytic causes bleeding during infusion. What is the most common complication?

Answer: C. Bleeding
Bleeding is the major/common fibrinolytic complication.
Q48Streptokinase

Which fibrinolytic is not fibrin-specific and is antigenic because it is a streptococcal protein?

Answer: A. Streptokinase
Streptokinase binds free and bound plasminogen, causing more systemic bleeding and allergic/fever reactions.
Q49tPA variants

Which fibrinolytics have higher fibrin specificity and single-bolus dosing?

Answer: A. Tenecteplase and reteplase
The lecture highlights tenecteplase/reteplase as higher fibrin-specific, bolus options.
Q50Fibrinolytic CI

A shark attack victim has recent brain surgery and active bleeding. Which class is most obviously contraindicated?

Answer: B. Fibrinolytics
Fibrinolytics dissolve clots and are contraindicated with major bleeding risks such as recent intracranial surgery/active bleeding.

Q9Mannitol MOA

Q10Mannitol route

Why is mannitol IV only?
