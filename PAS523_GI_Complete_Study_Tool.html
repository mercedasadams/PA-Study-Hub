Viral Hepatitis

Hepatitis A Lecture 5

Presentation
  • N/V/D, anorexia, FEVER, malaise, RUQ pain
  • Later: dark urine, pale stools, jaundice, pruritus
  • Incubation ~28 days average
  • Most patients: mild, self-limited
→
Diagnosis / Suspicion
  • Elevated AST/ALT (ALT usually 1000s+)
  • Hyperbilirubinemia
→
First Test
  • Liver function tests (AST/ALT, bilirubin)
→
Confirmatory Test
  • Serology: (+) IgM and anti-HAV antibodies
→
Treatment
  • Supportive care — usually resolves within a few months
  • Vaccine available: 2 doses, 6-12 months apart

Hepatitis B Lecture 5

Presentation
  • Malaise, fatigue, arthralgia, n/v/d, abd pain
  • Jaundice in only ~1/3 of patients
  • Dark urine, pale stools
  • Can progress to fulminant liver failure; acute or chronic phases
→
Diagnosis / Suspicion
  • Elevated AST/ALT (1000s acute, 100s chronic)
  • Hyperbilirubinemia
  • (+) HBV DNA
→
First Test
  • LFTs + HBV DNA
→
Confirmatory Test
  • Full serology panel: HBsAg, anti-HBs, anti-HBc (IgM/IgG), HBeAg, anti-HBe
  • Pattern distinguishes window period / acute / resolved / immunized / chronic (replicative vs. non-replicative)
→
Treatment
  • Supportive care — most do not progress to chronic disease
  • Antiviral if: persistent/severe symptoms, bilirubin >10, severe transaminitis, or HBeAg persistence
  • Entecavir & Tenofovir = first line; alternates: Adefovir, Telbivudine, Lamivudine
  • Continue until HBsAg clearance
  • Vaccine available (childhood series)

Hepatitis C Lecture 5

Presentation
  • Often asymptomatic until cirrhosis signs appear (ascites, varices, portal HTN)
  • Acute: fatigue, malaise, RUQ pain, jaundice, clay-colored stools
  • Incubation 6-7 weeks
→
Diagnosis / Suspicion
  • LFTs elevated, waxing and waning
  • CBC w/diff, PT/PTT/INR/fibrinogen
→
First Test
  • HCV antibody (anti-HCV) — positive result within 6 weeks
→
Confirmatory Test
  • HCV RNA
  • Anti-HCV without RNA = past recovery
  • Anti-HCV WITH RNA = likely chronic hepatitis
→
Treatment
  • Oral regimens first line: Glecaprevir-Pibrentasvir (8 wks) or Sofosbuvir-Velpatasvir (12 wks)
  • Check Hep B status first — treatment can reactivate Hep B
  • Recheck HCV levels ~3 months post-regimen
  • Close monitoring for cirrhosis/malignancy if signs present
MC cause of chronic liver disease/cirrhosis leading to transplant; chronic hepatitis occurs in as high as 85%

Hepatitis D Lecture 5

Presentation
  • Co-infection (new Hep B + Hep D together): clinically indistinguishable from acute Hep B, typically self-limiting
  • Superinfection (Hep D in known/unknown chronic Hep B carrier): severe hepatitis or exacerbation of chronic Hep B
  • Superinfection = higher risk of liver failure/cirrhosis and HCC
→
Diagnosis / Suspicion
  • Known/suspected Hep B + worsening hepatitis picture
→
First Test
  • Hep B serologies (HBsAg required for HDV infectivity)
→
Confirmatory Test
  • (+) Hepatitis D antigen (anti-HDV)
  • (+) Serum HDV RNA
  • IgM Hep B core antibody present only in co-infection
→
Treatment
  • Pegylated Interferon Alpha 2b — eliminates Hep D RNA in ~20-50% (significant side effects)
  • Liver transplant if liver failure/cirrhosis develops

Hepatitis E Lecture 5

Presentation
  • Jaundice (~5-10 days post-exposure), malaise, anorexia, n/v, abd pain, fever, hepatomegaly
  • Incubation 15-60 days
  • Most: self-limiting illness; fulminant hepatitis rare (~1-2%)
→
Diagnosis / Suspicion
  • Elevated AST/ALT (ALT usually 1000+)
  • Hyperbilirubinemia
→
First Test
  • Liver function tests
→
Confirmatory Test
  • Serology: (+) IgM and anti-HEV antibodies
→
Treatment
  • Supportive care
  • Chronic hepatitis can develop in immunocompromised patients
MC cause of acute viral hepatitis globally

Cirrhosis & Its Complications

Cirrhosis Lecture 5

Presentation
  • Fatigue, weakness, weight loss, abd pain
  • GI bleed (portal HTN/varices, PUD)
  • PE: ascites, spider angioma, telangiectasia, hepatosplenomegaly, gynecomastia, muscle wasting, jaundice, encephalopathy signs
→
Diagnosis / Suspicion
  • CMP: elevated LFTs, hyperbilirubinemia
  • Coagulation studies
→
First Test
  • US RUQ/Abd — fibrosis nodularity, increased echogenicity, atrophy/hypertrophy
  • Elastography — measures liver stiffness
→
Confirmatory Test
  • Liver biopsy = definitive diagnosis
→
Treatment
  • Restrict alcohol, hepatotoxic meds, sodium
  • Ascites/edema: diuretics (spironolactone/Lasix) ± intermittent paracentesis
  • Pruritus: Cholestyramine
  • Monitor closely for GI bleeding
  • LIVER TRANSPLANT = ULTIMATE CURE
Survival by class — A: 100%/85% (1yr/2yr); B: 81%/57%; C: 45%/35%

Hepatic Encephalopathy Lecture 5

Presentation
  • CNS symptoms: confusion, drowsiness, stupor, coma
  • PE: asterixis (flapping tremor w/ wrist extension)
  • PE: fetor hepaticus (foul breath)
→
Diagnosis / Suspicion
  • Known hepatic dysfunction + CNS findings above
→
First Test
  • Ammonia level
  • CMP: elevated LFTs, hyperbilirubinemia, low albumin
→
Confirmatory / Workup
  • Rule out precipitating trigger: CBC, kidney function, urine drug screen, coagulation studies, lactic acid/procalcitonin/blood cultures, consider CT head, abd US
→
Treatment
  • Lactulose or Lactitol = first line — gut flora converts to lactic/fatty acids, neutralizes ammonia
  • Rifaximin = alternative if lactulose not tolerated
  • Nutritional/electrolyte support, hydration
  • Monitor closely for recurrence; must rule out infection

NAFLD / NASH (MASH) Lecture 5

Presentation
  • Most asymptomatic
  • 75% have hepatomegaly
  • Risk factors: obesity (MC), DM, HLD, steroid use
→
Diagnosis / Suspicion
  • Abnormal LFTs, often found incidentally
→
First Test
  • CMP / coagulation studies / ammonia
  • Imaging showing hepatic steatosis
→
Confirmatory Test
  • Liver biopsy = definitive diagnosis — shows micro-vesicular fatty deposits
  • Distinguishes simple NAFL (triglyceride accumulation) vs. NASH/MASH (cell injury/death + inflammation → can progress to fibrosis/cirrhosis)
→
Treatment
  • Lifestyle modification first: weight loss, fat restriction, diet/exercise
  • Medical management reserved for stage 2+ fibrosis: GLP-1 therapies (Semaglutide, Tirzepatide, Liraglutide)
  • Resmetirom — alt. for GLP-1 intolerant (thyroid receptor beta agonist)
  • ION224 — investigational, blocks DGAT2
  • Liver transplant if cirrhosis develops

Portal Hypertension Lecture 5

Presentation
  • Often asymptomatic until complications occur
  • Hepatosplenomegaly, ascites, jaundice, abdominal distention
  • ABCDE: Ascites, Bleeding (hematemesis/piles), Caput medusae, Diminished liver (atrophy), Enlarged spleen
→
Diagnosis / Suspicion
  • Often diagnosed clinically
→
First Test
  • US with elastography — liver stiffness + other acute findings
→
Confirmatory Test
  • HVPG (Hepatic Venous Pressure Gradient)
  • Normal: 1-5 mmHg
  • Clinically significant if >10 mmHg
→
Treatment
  • Sodium/salt restriction
  • Diuretics (spironolactone, Lasix) ± intermittent paracentesis
  • Beta blockers: Carvedilol, Propranolol, Nadolol
  • TIPS — shunt to relieve portal pressure

Esophageal Varices & Acute Variceal Hemorrhage Lecture 2

Presentation
  • Chronic: typically asymptomatic until rupture; seen in advanced liver disease
  • Acute hemorrhage: hematemesis, active bleeding
→
Diagnosis / Suspicion
  • Pathophysiology: cirrhosis → resistance to portal flow → portal HTN → distal esophageal veins enlarge → rupture risk
→
First Test / Screening
  • EGD screening in newly diagnosed cirrhotics
→
Confirmatory Test
  • EGD — direct visualization of varices
  • During active bleed: emergent EGD after stabilization
→
Treatment
Primary prophylaxis (before bleeding)
  • Nonselective beta blockers (propranolol, nadolol, carvedilol)
Acute hemorrhage
  • Resuscitate!!! ABCs, IV fluids, pRBC transfusion
  • Octreotide — reduces portal HTN by inhibiting splanchnic vasodilation
  • Antibiotic prophylaxis: Ceftriaxone
  • PPI to reduce acid
  • Stabilize → emergent EGD
  • Temporize with balloon tamponade (Blakemore-Sengstaken tube)

Hepatocellular Carcinoma Lecture 5

Presentation
  • Most asymptomatic until progressive disease/metastasis
  • Malaise/weight loss, jaundice, abdominal pain, hepatosplenomegaly, ascites
→
Diagnosis / Suspicion
  • High-risk screening population: cirrhosis, hepatitis B history
→
First Test
  • US Abd (liver) every 6 months for surveillance
  • Lesion <1cm → repeat US every 3-6 months
→
Confirmatory Test
  • Lesion >1cm → CT A/P w/contrast or MRI w/wo contrast — arterial enhancement with venous washout
  • AFP can aid diagnosis/surveillance (non-specific)
  • Biopsy typically not required in high-risk patients meeting imaging criteria
→
Treatment
  • Surgery (resection) — no cirrhosis, tumor confined to one lobe
  • Liver transplant — curative option, must meet criteria
  • Early/limited disease: radiofrequency or microwave ablation
  • Intermediate disease: TACE (transarterial chemoembolization)
  • Progressive/metastatic: 1st line — Atezolizumab+Bevacizumab, Durvalumab+Tremelimumab, or Ipilimumab+Nivolumab; alt: Lenvatinib/Sorafenib
  • CAUTION: screen for esophageal varices before Atezolizumab+Bevacizumab (bleeding risk)

Biliary Disease

Cholelithiasis Lecture 5

Presentation
  • Majority asymptomatic
  • Biliary colic: RUQ/epigastric pain radiating to right shoulder blade, 30 min-few hours, ± nausea/diaphoresis, often after fatty meal
→
Diagnosis / Suspicion
  • Clinical picture + risk factors: female, pregnancy, age, HLD, OCPs, Native American descent, DM, HRT
→
First Test
  • US Abd/Liver = 1st diagnostic choice — stones appear as echogenic foci
  • CBC w/diff, CMP, amylase/lipase
→
Confirmatory Test
  • No separate confirmatory step in lecture — ultrasound is diagnostic
→
Treatment
  • Asymptomatic/incidental → monitor
  • Symptomatic (biliary colic) → cholecystectomy usually recommended
  • If declines surgery → educate thoroughly on complication risks

Cholecystitis Lecture 5

Presentation
  • RUQ/epigastric pain, fever, n/v, anorexia
  • PE: fever, tachycardia, + Murphy sign, palpable gallbladder, + Boas sign (referred right shoulder/subscapular pain)
→
Diagnosis / Suspicion
  • Clinical syndrome: RUQ pain + fever + leukocytosis, often with gallstone history
→
First Test
  • Abd US = initial 1st choice — gallbladder wall thickening/edema, distention, stones, +Murphy sign
  • CBC, CMP/LFTs, amylase/lipase, lactic acid, pregnancy test, blood cultures x2
→
Confirmatory Test
  • HIDA scan (radionuclide cholescintigraphy) — used if diagnosis unclear or gallbladder nonvisualized (cystic duct stone)
→
Treatment
  • IVF, electrolyte correction, pain control (NSAIDs), antiemetics
  • Antibiotics: Flagyl + Cephalosporin (Ceftriaxone/Cefazolin); high risk → Zosyn or Meropenem
  • Cholecystectomy preferred within 72 hours (ideally laparoscopic)
  • Watch for hemodynamic instability if perforation/gangrene suspected
  • High surgical risk → percutaneous cholecystostomy drainage

Choledocholithiasis Lecture 5

Presentation
  • Prolonged biliary colic: RUQ/epigastric pain sustained >6 hours, ± n/v, anorexia
  • PE: RUQ/epigastric tenderness, ± jaundice
→
Diagnosis / Suspicion
  • History of gallstones + prolonged pain/jaundice
  • Always consider in symptomatic patients with gallstone history
→
First Test
  • Abd US — dilated common bile duct
  • CMP: cholestatic pattern (elevated ALP + GGT), elevated AST/ALT; amylase/lipase
→
Confirmatory Test
  • ERCP = Diagnostic of CHOICE (diagnostic AND therapeutic)
  • MRCP = less-invasive alternative
→
Treatment
  • ERCP preferred — both diagnostic and therapeutic
  • Cholecystectomy can also be considered
  • Additional treatment if complicated by cholangitis/pancreatitis

Ascending Cholangitis Lecture 5

Presentation
  • Fever, chills, fatigue/malaise, rigors, abd pain, jaundice, pale stools
  • CHARCOT TRIAD: fever with chills, RUQ pain, jaundice
  • REYNOLDS PENTAD: + hypotension (shock), + AMS
  • "YOU MUST KNOW THESE!" — direct lecture emphasis
→
Diagnosis / Suspicion
  • Charcot triad / Reynolds pentad + biliary obstruction risk factors
→
First Test
  • Abd (RUQ) US = initial test of choice — CBD dilation/stones, bile duct wall thickening, pyogenic material
  • CBC (leukocytosis w/ left shift), CMP (elevated ALP/GGT, hyperbilirubinemia, elevated ammonia), coag studies, lactic acid/procalcitonin, blood cultures x2
  • CT if US inconclusive
→
Confirmatory Test
  • Cholangiography = GOLD STANDARD — ERCP
  • PTC (percutaneous transhepatic cholangiography) can also be considered
→
Treatment
  • IV antibiotics + IVF + antiemetics
  • ERCP: endoscopic sphincterotomy with stone extraction and/or stent for biliary drainage
  • PCT (percutaneous cholecystostomy tube) if unstable, can't tolerate ERCP, or ERCP unsuccessful
  • Open surgical decompression + drain if other methods fail

Appendicitis Lecture 3

Presentation
  • Vague periumbilical pain (visceral) → migrates to RLQ within ~24 hours
  • Anorexia, nausea, vomiting (pain typically precedes vomiting)
  • Low-grade fever
  • Only ~40% present with all classic features
→
Diagnosis / Suspicion
  • McBurney point, Rovsing, psoas, obturator signs; rebound tenderness
  • Leukocytosis (WBC >10,000 in 67-90%), left shift
  • β-hCG mandatory in women of reproductive age
→
First Test
  • CT abd/pelvis w/ IV contrast — adults, first-line (96%/96%)
  • US first-line in children/pregnant (no radiation)
→
Confirmatory Test
  • CT: dilated appendix ≥7mm, wall thickening, fat stranding, appendicolith
  • MRI if CT contraindicated (pregnancy)
→
Treatment
  • Laparoscopic appendectomy = gold standard
  • Antibiotics-first option for selected uncomplicated cases (~70% success; 30-40% eventually need surgery within 5 years)
  • Abscess/phlegmon: IV antibiotics ± percutaneous drainage, interval appendectomy (esp. age ≥35, to rule out neoplasm)
  • Perforation + peritonitis: emergent surgery

Celiac Disease Lecture 3

Presentation
  • Chronic diarrhea, bloating, abd pain, constipation, steatorrhea, weight loss
  • Iron-deficiency anemia refractory to oral supplementation; osteoporosis
  • Dermatitis herpetiformis — pruritic papulovesicular rash, extensor surfaces
→
Diagnosis / Suspicion
  • HLA-DQ2/DQ8 + gluten exposure
  • Screen high-risk: 1st-degree relatives, T1DM, autoimmune thyroid disease, Down/Turner/Williams syndrome
→
First Test
  • tTG-IgA + total serum IgA (check total IgA to catch selective IgA deficiency causing false negatives)
→
Confirmatory Test
  • Upper endoscopy w/ ≥4 duodenal biopsies — villous atrophy, crypt hyperplasia, >25 IELs
  • EMA-IgA near 100% specificity
  • Children: can skip biopsy if tTG-IgA ≥10x ULN + positive EMA
→
Treatment
  • Strict lifelong gluten-free diet — only effective treatment
  • Dietitian referral; monitor tTG-IgA for adherence
  • Baseline/f/u: CBC, iron/B12/folate/A/D/E, LFTs, TSH, DEXA
Testing must occur on a gluten-containing diet — GFD before testing causes false negatives

Intussusception Lecture 3

Presentation
  • Classic triad: intermittent colicky abd pain, currant jelly stool, palpable sausage-shaped mass
  • Vomiting (often nonbilious initially)
  • Atypical: lethargy/AMS in infant — high-yield pearl
→
Diagnosis / Suspicion
  • Peak age 5-9 months; ileocolic = MC type
→
First Test
  • Ultrasound = diagnostic modality of choice (sensitivity >98%)
  • Target/doughnut sign (transverse), pseudo-kidney sign (longitudinal)
→
Confirmatory Test
  • Ultrasound itself is diagnostic — no separate confirmatory test
→
Treatment
  • Medical/surgical emergency
  • First-line: non-operative enema reduction (pneumatic air preferred), pressure 80-120 mmHg, "rule of threes"; success ~66-71%
  • Surgery if: unstable/peritonitic, perforation, failed enema, pathologic lead point

Small Bowel Obstruction (SBO) Lecture 3

Presentation
  • Classic tetrad: colicky abd pain, N/V (bilious vs feculent), abd distension, obstipation
  • High-pitched "tinkling" bowel sounds early → absent late
→
Diagnosis / Suspicion
  • Adhesions (65%, MC — prior surgery), hernias (10%, highest strangulation ~33%), neoplasm, Crohn's
  • Red flags for strangulation: fever, tachycardia, hypotension, rigidity
→
First Test
  • Abd X-ray (KUB) — dilated loops with air-fluid levels; cannot exclude SBO
→
Confirmatory Test
  • CT abd/pelvis w/ IV contrast = gold standard
  • Dilated proximal bowel (>3cm) with transition point; ischemia signs (decreased wall enhancement, pneumatosis, portal venous gas)
→
Treatment
  • Nonoperative (65-80% success, esp. partial): NPO, IVF, NGT decompression, Gastrografin (diagnostic + therapeutic)
  • Surgery if: peritonitis/strangulation, closed-loop obstruction, irreducible hernia, complete obstruction, or failure by 3-5 days

Paralytic (Adynamic) Ileus Lecture 4

Presentation
  • Similar to SBO but slower onset: abd pain, N/V, obstipation, distention, fullness
  • Decreased/absent bowel sounds, tympany, mild tenderness
→
Diagnosis / Suspicion
  • Postoperative = MC cause
  • Opioids/antidiarrheals, electrolyte abnormalities (hypokalemia, hypercalcemia, hypomagnesemia), severe hypothyroidism, DM
→
First Test
  • Abd X-ray — diffusely dilated bowel loops WITHOUT a transition zone, paucity of gas in colon/rectum
→
Confirmatory Test
  • CT A/P confirms diagnosis — differentiates mechanical obstruction vs. ileus
→
Treatment
  • Supportive: NPO → advance diet as tolerated, IVF/electrolyte replacement, serial abd films/girths, NGT decompression
  • Most resolve in 1-3 days; watch for perforation/peritonitis/sepsis if persistent

Crohn Disease Lecture 4

Presentation
  • Crampy abd pain, chronic diarrhea without gross blood, fatigue/weight loss (malabsorption)
  • Ileocolitis: RLQ pain, diarrhea, fatigue, weight loss, fever, anorexia
  • Severe: perianal disease, abscess, fistula
→
Diagnosis / Suspicion
  • Transmural inflammation, can affect any part of GI tract
  • Terminal ileum MC segment; rectum usually spared; skip lesions
→
First Test
  • Ileocolonoscopy with biopsy — segmental disease, cobblestoning, aphthous ulcerations, strictures
→
Confirmatory Test
  • Biopsy: transmural inflammation, focal lesions ± noncaseating granulomas
  • +anti-Saccharomyces cerevisiae Ab, iron/B12 deficiency, elevated CRP/ESR
  • Enterography MRI preferred imaging (avoids radiation)
→
Treatment
  • Step-up, immune-directed; limited/right colon: oral glucocorticoids ± oral 5-ASA
  • Severe/refractory: immunomodulators (Azathioprine, 6-MP, MTX), biologic anti-TNF (Adalimumab, Infliximab)
  • Taper meds over time; f/u endoscopy 6-12 months

Ulcerative Colitis Lecture 4

Presentation
  • Hematochezia, diarrhea with blood, LLQ abd pain, increased urgency, tenesmus
  • Systemic symptoms if severe: fever, fatigue, malaise, weight loss
→
Diagnosis / Suspicion
  • Inflammation limited to mucosal/submucosal layers
  • Involves rectum, extends proximally in a continuous pattern; 40-50% limited to rectum/rectosigmoid
→
First Test
  • Flexible sigmoidoscopy/colonoscopy — uniform mucosal erythema, granularity, ulceration
  • Avoid colonoscopy during a severe flare
→
Confirmatory Test
  • Biopsy: goblet depletion, mucosal lymphocyte/plasma cell infiltration
  • Barium enema: stovepipe/lead pipe sign
  • +p-ANCA, elevated ESR/CRP, anemia of chronic disease
→
Treatment
  • Mild-mod right colon: topical 5-ASA first → topical steroid → oral 5-ASA → oral steroids
  • Left-sided/extensive: combo oral+topical 5-ASA upfront → topical steroid → Budesonide MMX → oral steroids
  • Refractory: anti-TNF, anti-integrin (Vedolizumab); severe hospitalized bleeding: IV steroids

Diverticulosis Lecture 4

Presentation
  • Often asymptomatic
  • MC cause of acute lower GI bleeding (painless hematochezia)
→
Diagnosis / Suspicion
  • Sac-like protrusions, MC in left colon; right colon = MC bleeding site (thinner walls)
  • Risk factors: male, smoking, obesity, constipation, low fiber, age, NSAID/aspirin use
→
First Test
  • Colonoscopy 1st line (after UGIB ruled out)
→
Confirmatory Test
  • Radionuclide imaging (tagged RBC scan) if bleeding source unclear
→
Treatment
  • Bleeding stops spontaneously most of the time
  • Lifestyle: high fiber diet, psyllium
  • Endoscopic therapy (epinephrine injection) if significant bleeding

Diverticulitis Lecture 4

Presentation
  • LLQ abd pain, N/V, constipation or diarrhea
  • PE: LLQ tenderness, rebound, guarding, palpable if abscess
→
Diagnosis / Suspicion
  • Inflammation/necrosis of diverticula (micro/macroperforation)
  • Sigmoid colon = MC location
→
First Test
  • CT with contrast = 1st line imaging — wall thickening >4mm, pericolonic fat stranding
  • Colonoscopy CONTRAINDICATED (perforation risk)
→
Confirmatory Test
  • CT itself is diagnostic; classifies complicated (abscess/perforation/obstruction/fistula) vs. uncomplicated
→
Treatment
  • ~70% treated without complications
  • Uncomplicated: outpatient PO Metronidazole + Cipro/Levo x7-10 days, advance diet
  • Complicated: inpatient IV antibiotics; surgical drainage if abscess >3-4cm; surgery if refractory/recurrent/perforated

Toxic Megacolon Lecture 4

Presentation
  • Severe bloody diarrhea, abd pain, N/V, tenesmus
  • Fever, tachycardia, hypotension, dehydration, AMS, peritonitis signs
→
Diagnosis / Suspicion
  • UC complication, infectious colitis (C. diff, CMV, Salmonella, Shigella, Campylobacter), radiation, CRC
→
First Test
  • Abd X-ray and/or CT A/P — colonic dilation >6cm
→
Confirmatory Test
  • Dilation >6cm plus ≥3 of (fever>38C, HR>120, WBC>10K, anemia) plus ≥1 of (dehydration, AMS, electrolyte disturbance, hypotension)
→
Treatment
  • IVF/electrolytes, bowel rest (NPO), broad-spectrum IV antibiotics (Ceftriaxone + Flagyl), NGT decompression
  • Avoid antimotility agents
  • Treat cause (UC→IV steroids; CMV→IV Ganciclovir; C.diff→PO Vancomycin/Fidaxomicin)
  • Perforation → emergent colectomy

Acute Mesenteric Ischemia Lecture 4

Presentation
  • Severe abd pain out of proportion to PE findings, poorly localized
  • N/V/diarrhea; advanced: peritonitis/shock/sepsis
→
Diagnosis / Suspicion
  • Arterial occlusion (embolism, A-fib MC; SMA MC site), non-occlusive (sepsis/shock/MI/CHF/cocaine), venous thrombosis
  • ~60% mortality
→
First Test
  • CTA 1st line if stable (without oral contrast)
  • Labs: leukocytosis, elevated lactic acid, elevated LDH
→
Confirmatory Test
  • Catheter-based arteriography — confirmatory + therapeutic (vasodilators, angioplasty, thrombolytic)
→
Treatment
  • RAPID REVASCULARIZATION
  • Embolism: surgical embolectomy ± thrombolytics
  • Arterial thrombosis: surgical bypass or endovascular angioplasty/stent
  • Venous thrombosis: systemic anticoagulation/thrombolytics
  • Perforation: emergent laparotomy + bowel resection

Chronic Mesenteric Ischemia Lecture 4

Presentation
  • Chronic dull abd pain worse after meals ("intestinal angina"), anorexia, weight loss
  • PE: mild tenderness, abdominal bruit in up to 50%
→
Diagnosis / Suspicion
  • Atherosclerotic narrowing of 2-3 splanchnic vessels (celiac, SMA, IMA)
  • Risk factors: atherosclerosis, DM, HTN, smoking
→
First Test
  • CTA 1st line imaging
→
Confirmatory Test
  • Conventional angiography — confirmatory diagnostic
→
Treatment
  • Asymptomatic: lifestyle changes, antiplatelet therapy
  • Symptomatic: revascularization (open or endovascular bypass/stenting)

Ischemic Colitis Lecture 4

Presentation
  • Rapid-onset abd pain over affected region (left-sided/LLQ MC)
  • Bloody diarrhea within 24h of pain onset
→
Diagnosis / Suspicion
  • Mostly elderly (90% are 60+); "watershed area" ischemia, usually mucosal not transmural
  • 95% non-occlusive (systemic hypotension/atherosclerosis)
→
First Test
  • CT Abdomen/Pelvis = 1st line imaging — wall edema, "thumbprinting", double halo/target sign
→
Confirmatory Test
  • Sigmoidoscopy/colonoscopy CONFIRMS diagnosis (contraindicated if high risk/active perforation)
→
Treatment
  • Supportive first — most respond: restore perfusion, bowel rest (NPO), IVF, consider antibiotics
  • Surgical resection if colonic infarction/necrosis

Irritable Bowel Syndrome (IBS) Lecture 4

Presentation
  • Abd pain/cramping, associated with defecation, may be relieved by defecation
  • Subtypes: IBS-C, IBS-D, IBS-M, IBS-U; exacerbated by stress/certain foods
→
Diagnosis / Suspicion
  • Disorder of brain-gut interaction; assoc. w/ fibromyalgia, chronic fatigue, GERD, psychiatric disorders
  • PE usually benign
→
First Test
  • Diagnosis of exclusion — rule out infection, celiac disease, etc.
→
Confirmatory Test

Acute Pancreatitis Lecture 5

Presentation
  • Epigastric pain radiating to the back, worse supine/with eating, relieved leaning forward/sitting/fetal position
  • Fever, N/V, abd distention; shock symptoms if severe
  • Necrotizing (hemorrhagic): Cullen's sign (periumbilical ecchymosis), Grey Turner sign (flank ecchymosis)
→
Diagnosis / Suspicion
  • Gallstones = MC etiology, alcohol abuse also MC
  • Meds: Thiazides, Protease Inhibitors, Estrogens, Exenatide, Valproic Acid
→
First Test
  • Lipase/Amylase elevated ≥3x ULN (lipase more specific)
  • Hypocalcemia (necrotic fat binds calcium), hyperglycemia, elevated lactic acid/procalcitonin
→
Confirmatory Test
  • Diagnostic criteria (need 2 of 3): acute severe epigastric pain radiating to back, lipase/amylase ≥3x ULN, characteristic imaging findings
  • CT A/P w/contrast = diagnostic of choice — enlarged pancreas, indistinct margins; imaging not required if first two criteria met
  • Abd US to assess for gallstones/bile duct dilation
→
Treatment
  • "REST THE PANCREAS": NPO, aggressive IVF (10mL/kg bolus) + electrolytes, pain control, insulin, antiemetics
  • Consider TPN; antibiotics not typically needed unless infection suspected
  • Advance diet as tolerated over 2-3 days; ERCP if indicated (gallstone etiology)

Presentation

→
Diagnosis / Suspicion

Gastric Adenocarcinoma (Correa Cascade) Lecture 3

Presentation
  • Often asymptomatic in early stages; dyspepsia; alarm features (weight loss, GI bleeding/anemia, dysphagia, persistent vomiting) in advanced disease
→
Diagnosis / Suspicion
  • The Correa Cascade: Normal tissue → Chronic gastritis → Atrophic gastritis → Intestinal metaplasia → Dysplasia → Gastric adenocarcinoma
  • H. pylori responsible for majority of noncardiac gastric cancers; chronic atrophic gastritis increases risk
→
First Test
  • EGD with biopsy (Sydney protocol — 5 sites) in patients with alarm features or age ≥60 with new dyspepsia
→
Confirmatory Test
  • OLGA staging (Operative Link on Gastritis Assessment) stratifies cancer risk — stages III-IV carry significantly elevated neoplasia risk; stages 0-II near-zero risk
  • Histology = gold standard
→
Treatment
  • H. pylori eradication reduces progression risk (see Stomach & Duodenal tab for regimens)
  • Surveillance intensifies with OLGA stage

Colonic Polyps Lecture 4

Presentation
  • Typically asymptomatic; can bleed or cause obstruction
→
Diagnosis / Suspicion
  • Types: inflammatory/pseudopolyps (UC/Crohn's), serrated (hyperplastic = MC non-neoplastic; SSLs/TSAs = increased cancer risk)

→
First Test

→
Confirmatory Test
  • Histology on resected specimen
→
Treatment

Colorectal Cancer (CRC) Lecture 4

Presentation
  • Change in bowel habits = MC symptom (74%), esp. left-sided tumors; hematochezia (right-sided/rectosigmoid); iron-deficiency anemia (right-sided); abd pain (all locations); rectal cancer: tenesmus, rectal pain
  • 1 in 5 present with metastatic disease (liver MC site)
→
Diagnosis / Suspicion
  • Screening avg-risk: age 45-75, colonoscopy q10 years or annual FIT
  • High-risk (Lynch/FAP FDR): start age 40 or 10yr before relative's diagnosis, colonoscopy q5 years
  • Lynch syndrome: MC CRC susceptibility syndrome, AD, suspect if CRC <age 50; FAP: APC gene, cancer by age 20 if untreated; Peutz-Jeghers: STK11, hamartomas
→
First Test
  • Colonoscopy with biopsy = diagnostic of choice
  • CT for generalized abd symptoms; CT colonography if incomplete colonoscopy
→
Confirmatory Test
  • Biopsy confirms histology (adenocarcinoma 85%, mucinous 12%)
  • Staging: CT TAP for metastasis, CEA/CA19-9 tumor markers (not for screening — CEA >5ng/mL preop = worse prognosis)
→
Treatment
  • Stage 0-1: surgery only. Stage 2: surgery + chemo (FOLFOX/CapeOx) for rectal/high-risk colon + radiation for rectal. Stage 3: surgery + FOLFOX/CapeOx + radiation (rectal). Stage 4: surgery if obstructive, systemic chemo (FOLFOX/FOLFIRI/FOLFIRINOX + biologics), possible IR (RFA/SBRT/chemoembolization)
  • Surveillance: H&P + CEA q3-6mo x5yr, CT TAP annually x3-5yr, colonoscopy at 1yr then q5yr

Pancreatic Malignancies Lecture 5

Presentation
  • Asthenia, weight loss, anorexia, abd/epigastric pain, back pain, N/V, steatorrhea, dark urine (early jaundice sign)
  • Painless jaundice (55%), hepatomegaly, Courvoisier's sign (palpable nontender distended gallbladder)
  • Metastatic signs: Virchow's node (left supraclavicular), Sister Mary Joseph's node (periumbilical), Trousseau syndrome (migratory thrombophlebitis)
→
Diagnosis / Suspicion
  • 85% adenocarcinoma (ductal epithelium, ~6mo avg survival); also ampullary/duodenal carcinoma, cystadenoma/cystocarcinoma (better prognosis), NETs (~5%)
  • Third leading cause of cancer death in U.S.; 60-70% occur in pancreatic head
  • Risk factors: smoking (2x risk), obesity (BMI>30, +20% risk), DM, chronic pancreatitis history, family history
→
First Test
  • Abd US to rule out mass/bile duct obstruction/liver abnormalities
  • CT scan w/contrast = best initial diagnostic of choice
→
Confirmatory Test
  • Biopsy required to confirm diagnosis — EUS-guided FNA = best modality
  • ERCP (diagnostic + therapeutic) or MRCP as alternative; CA19-9 tumor marker (poor specificity/sensitivity, used for staging/postop monitoring)
  • Staging via TNM, CT/PET for metastasis
→
Treatment
  • Head/duodenal region tumor → Whipple procedure (pancreaticoduodenectomy), ~20-25% 5-year survival
  • Tail tumors → distal pancreatectomy; total pancreatectomy → lifelong insulin/enzyme replacement
  • ~40% present unresectable → systemic chemo ± radiation; some become resectable after neoadjuvant chemo (borderline resectable)
